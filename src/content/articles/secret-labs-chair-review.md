If you spend six or more hours a day at a desk, your chair is not a luxury — it is a productivity tool. I have been sitting in a Secret Labs chair for a while now, and here is the honest take.

## Why Secret Labs?

Secret Labs started as a gaming chair company and has gradually pulled the ergonomics up to meet actual office use. The result is a chair that looks good on a stream, holds up under daily professional use, and does not fall apart in two years the way most budget options do.

The build quality is immediately obvious out of the box. The frame is solid steel, the recline mechanism has real resistance adjustment, and the lumbar support is adjustable both in height and depth — something most chairs in this price range skip entirely.

## What I use it for

I run client calls, long writing sessions, and the occasional three-hour deep-work block from home. Back fatigue was a real issue before switching. After? Noticeably better. The lumbar and neck pillow are not afterthoughts — they actually work.

## Who it is not for

- If you are under 5'4" or significantly lighter than average, fit can be tricky. Secret Labs publishes a sizing guide and it is worth reading before you order.
- If you want a neutral, non-gaming aesthetic, some models lean heavily into the gaming look. The Titan Evo line is their most office-friendly.

## Pricing

Models generally run from around **$399 to $599 USD**, depending on size and material (fabric, SoftWeave, or NAPA leather). Not cheap. But compared to an Aeron or a Steelcase Leap, it is still a significant discount for comparable core ergonomics.
