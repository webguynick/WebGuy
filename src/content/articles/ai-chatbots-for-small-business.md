Every small business owner has been pitched an AI chatbot this year. Half of them are worth it. Half are a monthly subscription you will forget about. Here is how to tell the difference.

## What a good AI chatbot actually does

A 2026 chatbot is not a decision tree. It reads your site, your FAQ, your service list, and sometimes your Google Business Profile, then answers customer questions in plain language. The best ones hand off to a real human when it gets stuck.

Use cases that pay for themselves on a small business site:

1. **Qualifying leads after hours.** Instead of a contact form blackhole at 9pm, the bot asks two follow up questions, captures the email, and texts you.
2. **Answering the same five questions.** Hours, parking, price ranges, booking rules. If you answer the same thing in DMs every day, a bot removes that tax.
3. **Booking appointments.** Bot collects the preferred time, drops it on your calendar, confirms by email.

## What to budget

Realistic numbers for a small business, not enterprise:

- Entry level tools like Chatbase, Tidio, or Intercom Fin: **$29 to $99 a month**.
- Custom Claude or OpenAI integration on your own site: **$200 to $600 one time** to set up, plus pennies per conversation.
- Full AI voice agent (phone): **$100 to $400 a month** depending on minutes.

## The three traps

1. **Hallucinations.** If the bot does not know, it guesses. Lock it to answering only from your documents. Every reputable platform supports this in 2026. Turn it on.
2. **No handoff.** A bot that cannot escalate to a human kills more leads than it saves. If the vendor does not offer SMS or email handoff, skip them.
3. **Drowning in transcripts.** If you never review what the bot says, it slowly drifts off brand. Read one week of transcripts per month. It takes 15 minutes.

## When to skip the chatbot

If your site gets fewer than 50 visitors a day, you do not need a bot. You need an obvious phone number and a short contact form. Skip it for now and revisit when traffic grows.
